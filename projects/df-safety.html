Research signal sources
6 key insights → UX decisions
Insight 1 AI trust requires transparency
Reviewers won't accept AI suggestions without a confidence score and "why" tooltip — explainability UI is mandatory
Insight 2 Regulatory compliance = UX constraint #1
Every screen must support E2B R2/R3 audit trail — accept/correct decisions must be logged with timestamp and user
Insight 3 MedDRA lookup is the biggest time sink
Reviewers navigate 5 hierarchy levels manually → NLP auto-suggestion + one-click accept is the primary UX win
Insight 4 5 vigilance categories = 5 different workflows (currently)
CRO teams switch contexts constantly — a unified UX framework with category-specific modules reduces cognitive load
Insight 5 Signal detection needs executive-readable charts
Signal data exists but is presented as raw tables — actionable dashboards with risk tiers are what safety leads actually need
Insight 6 Bulk XML migration is a CRO dealbreaker
Large CROs onboard with thousands of existing cases — bulk XML import/export UX must be frictionless or the platform fails at adoption

Phase 08 Challenges, Learnings & V2 Modifications Ongoing
Challenge 1 — Balancing AI explainability with audit trail

Reviewers needed to understand why the AI flagged a case as serious — but every explanation also had to be logged in the E2B audit trail. Solved with a collapsible "AI reasoning" tooltip that writes to the audit log on expand — explainability without cluttering the primary workflow.

Challenge 2 — 5 vigilance categories with different data models

Cosmetics and nutraceuticals don't use MedDRA the same way drugs do — but the navigation had to feel unified. Solved with a configurable "product type" setting that slots category-specific modules into a consistent shell — reviewers see the same UX patterns regardless of category.

Learning 1 — Regulatory design is product design

Every UX decision — button placement, field order, confirmation dialogs — had a regulatory implication. Learning to read ICH E2B guidelines as a UX designer, not just a compliance checkbox, made every design decision defensible and faster to get signed off.

Learning 2 — AI trust is earned through transparency, not accuracy

Even when the ML model was 94% accurate, reviewers wouldn't click "accept" without seeing the confidence score and understanding the reasoning. The UX work of making AI trustworthy — not the model itself — was the primary lever for adoption.

V2 modifications — planned next iterations
V2 · Multi-modal case intake

Extend AI auto-creation to support PDF, image (JPEG/PNG), and even handwritten document import — converting unstructured case narratives directly into structured ICSR fields via NLP. The ML infrastructure already supports this; the UX needs upload + validation flows.

V2 · Literature surveillance module

Auto-connect to PubMed for literature screening — AI triage of articles to flag potential pharmacovigilance cases. UX design: a reading-list dashboard where reviewers see pre-triaged articles, click to confirm or dismiss, and auto-create cases from confirmed ones.

V2 · Expanded device + cosmetic modules

Current device and cosmetic UX adapts the drug framework — V2 would build purpose-designed workflows for MoCRA 2022 (US cosmetic reporting) and EU MDR device vigilance requirements, with category-specific AI models and dedicated signal detection logic.
