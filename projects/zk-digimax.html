5 key insights that shaped every product decision
1
Bulk updates = 70% of all tasks. Staff spent most time on repetitive mass-pricing tasks, not individual SKU edits. One-click bulk action was the highest-priority feature by far.
2
Navigation is the hidden time killer. 25% of inventory search time was walking to find items. QR/NFC-based indoor navigation was not on the original brief — interviews surfaced it.
3
Competitors win on API flexibility, not UI. Hanshow and Pricer offered open APIs — ZKDigiMax needed to match this AND beat them on mobile UX to win India/Nigeria retail segments.
4
Wi-Fi reliability was the #1 deployment risk. 8 of 15 stores had patchy in-store Wi-Fi. Offline-first architecture was added to requirements after discovery — this prevented a post-launch failure.
5
Managers need a live dashboard, not just an app. Store ops and regional managers wanted real-time visibility of tag status — not just a mobile tool for floor staff. Dual audience confirmed.

07 — Challenges & Learnings

What went wrong, how it was fixed, and what it taught me

Challenge 1 — Wi-Fi failure risk
8 of 15 discovery stores had unreliable in-store Wi-Fi. A standard sync-only architecture would have failed at launch.
Fix: Added offline-first queue architecture — updates cached locally, synced on reconnect. Added to requirements after discovery. Zero offline failures at pilot.
Challenge 2 — Dual audience tension
Floor staff needed a fast, simple bulk tool. Managers needed a rich dashboard. One app, two very different UX needs.
Fix: Role-based app entry — staff login shows bulk actions first, manager login shows dashboard. 6 usability tests confirmed both flows before build.
Challenge 3 — Handoff knowledge loss risk
Handing source to China HQ with no documentation would mean all research and design rationale is lost permanently.
Fix: Comprehensive handoff package — PRD, Figma files, architecture diagrams, user research summaries, API docs, and a 45-min walkthrough video recorded for China team.
Key learning — Discovery changes everything
QR/NFC navigation was not in the original brief. It emerged from interviews. Without structured discovery, this would have shipped as version 2 — not day 1.
Proof: Navigation feature became the #2 most used feature in pilot stores, after bulk pricing. Brief ≠ user need.
